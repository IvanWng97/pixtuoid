# pixtuoid-scene — where to look

Answers for the questions indexed in [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md)'s
"Where to look". Each entry is self-contained — grep for your question
and read that one, or read the file end to end when mapping the crate.



- "Where does a furniture's footprint / visual size / approach side / dwell come from?" → **`layout::decor`** (this crate) is the single source of truth, and there is now exactly ONE geometry table. `FurnitureDef { footprint, visual, occupies_pos, exclusive, dwell, approach: ApproachSides }` + `furniture_def(Furniture)` — keyed by the unified `Furniture` geometry enum — plus `desk_furniture_def()` (the home desk: same `FurnitureDef` type, NOT a `Furniture`/waypoint row, since it's owned per-agent). **The role enums carry NO dimensions**: `WaypointKind` (wander destination — a NEW kind must also join or opt out of the `--anim`/`--crop-furniture` vocabulary census in `examples/snapshot/scenes.rs`, compile-enforced), `PodDecor` (aisle filler), `PlantKind`, `WallDecor` each expose `.furniture()` mapping onto one `Furniture` row, so overlapping items (a phone booth that is both a waypoint and pod decor, a plant that is both free-standing and pod decor, a whiteboard that is both wall and pod decor) have their shape defined exactly once. The singleton / per-room furniture (meeting sofa-body + table, the kitchen island, lamp, side table) are ALSO rows now — `Furniture::{MeetingSofaBody, MeetingTable, KitchenIsland, FloorLamp, LoungeSideTable}` — distinct from the `MeetingSofa`/`MeetingChair`/`IslandStand` SEAT rows (slots sit ON one body, so the slots carry `None` footprint and the body carries the obstacle). The ONLY geometry living outside the table is `PLANT_FOOTPRINT` (6×3, shared by only the TWO 6px-pot plants — Ficus + Tall; Flower (2×2) and Succulent (3×2) have their own shallow south-anchored pot strips, and a decor test pins that de-shared plants stay NARROWER than `PLANT_FOOTPRINT`) and the linear wall thickness `WALL_THICK_H/V` (walls are a thickness, not a point-centred rect). **`footprint` is the BLOCKED ground area** (z=0 projection) — `mask::build_walkable_mask` stamps it (no inline literals) at the row's DECLARED `ground_x`/`ground_y` (`GroundAlign` FIELDS on `FurnitureDef` as of 0.15.0 — the pub-field add on the published 0.14.0 struct is why this PR bumps the workspace minor); **`visual` is the sprite size** — render centering + the z-sort south row (`center_pin_south_offset`; the desk's z is `desk.y + visual.h`, a VISUAL property, so footprint edits are z-neutral) derive from it. **Four load-bearing distinctions:** (1) `footprint` (blocked ground) ≠ `visual` (sprite, may overhang — `Furniture::PlantTall` footprint 6×**3** (a shallow south-anchored pot strip) vs visual 6×10; overhanging pieces have a footprint *shorter* than the sprite so the canopy/column occludes a walker behind it via z-sort; the vertical divider wall rides the SAME `ground_rect` knob — `footprint ⊂ visual` with a north walk-behind cap (`WALL_TOP_OVERHANG_PX`) at a FREE terminus (a run below a door), so a character parked behind its top is occluded by the y-sorted `RoomWallV` drawable, exactly like the desk; a JOINTED terminus (window band OR a crossing horizontal wall — anything `stitch_vertical_wall` raised) drops the cap to `footprint == visual` full coverage, or the overhang would leave a walkable hole between the two walls); (2) `occupies_pos` (a RENDER fact: agent sits/stands ON the cell — couch/meeting — vs approaches from a side — appliances) ≠ `exclusive` (a CAPACITY fact: single-occupancy, claim-gated). `exclusive` is a SUPERSET of `occupies_pos` (`occupies_pos ⇒ exclusive`), and they DIVERGE for the phone booth + standing desk — stand-beside singles that render at a side cell yet hold one occupant (`occupies_pos: false, exclusive: true`); that divergence is exactly why capacity can't ride `occupies_pos` (the wrong-abstraction split — see the SpotClaims sharp edge); (3) **placement overlap** (furniture buried in a wall = bug, swept by `placement_sweep::no_furniture_ground_overlaps_a_wall`) ≠ **occlusion overlap** (glass-over-sofa, sofa-over-sitter, canopy-over-walker = physically correct, resolved by the tui z-sort, NOT a placement error); (4) `dwell` (per-spot idle window; `DECOR` sentinel = not a wander destination) is orthogonal to all of the above. `ApproachSides` (canonical facing-South, rotated by `allows`) is the editable entry-side knob — flip one bool to drop a side. The unifier is "one geometry table (footprint + visual) + one shared z-sort," not a second `size()` per role enum.
- "Why doesn't a bigger buffer just render the office sharper?" → because until `render_scale.rs` it COULDN'T: `layout::Point` is a buffer pixel, so `floor_capacity(buf_w, buf_h)` reads resolution as size. `RenderScale` is the seam that separates them, and `render_scale::tests::floor_capacity_is_invariant_under_render_scale` pins the property (a richer visual profile must not change desk count or assignment). Its sibling `without_the_seam_a_bigger_buffer_builds_a_bigger_office` is the negative control — it asserts the un-seamed call still balloons, so the invariant can't silently lose its teeth.
- "How is the office laid out?" → `layout::SceneLayout::compute_with_seed` (this crate) is the coordinator; it calls private helpers: `compute_pod_desks` (pod grid), `compute_pod_decor` (aisle decor), `rooms::walls::derive_room_walls` (request-based wall segments + door gaps, derived FROM the room aggregates), `compute_waypoints` (all waypoint assembly). `layout::Layout` is the compat alias call sites construct through. **The desk count is uncapped in production**: the 3rd param is `max_desks: Option<usize>` — `None` (every production painter) FILLS the buffer with as many 2×2 desk pods as physically fit, `Some(n)` caps the count (the deterministic knob for tests/goldens/snapshots; `TEST_DEFAULT_DESKS = 16` survives only as the named test default, not an enforced limit). `pose::pure::derive` for pure state→pose mapping including the Idle wander state machine (cycle timing is the per-spot `dwell_ms`/`seated_dwell_ms` + `est_wander_cycle_ms`; `STALE_RESUME_GAP_BASE_MS=7000`+jitter is only the off-screen-resume sentinel, NOT the cycle).
- "How does walk-pace physics work?" → `pixtuoid_scene::physics` (pure, no terminal deps) — `WalkIntent` tags the walk kind (Entry/Exit/WanderOut/WanderBack/SnapBack); `walk_profile(path_len_octile, intent, agent_id)` returns a frozen `WalkProfile` with trapezoidal/triangular kinematics (triangular when path < `v²/a`, trapezoidal otherwise); `walk_progress(p, elapsed_ms)` emits `t_x1000 ∈ [0,1000]`; `walk_arrived` gates the Walking→Seated/AtWaypoint flip including the per-agent arrival pause. Constants: `V_CRUISE_COMMUTE = 0.36` octile/ms (Entry/Exit), `V_CRUISE_SNAPBACK = 0.65` octile/ms (SnapBack — the urgent Idle→Active return rushes back), `V_CRUISE_WANDER = 0.25` octile/ms (amble, wander legs), `WALK_ACCEL = 6.5e-4` octile/ms² (~0.55 s ramp; `L_crit ≈ 199` octile commute, `≈ 96` octile wander), `WALK_ACCEL_SNAPBACK = 2.0e-3` octile/ms² (~3× — short snap-backs are accel-limited, so the rush comes from a harder accelerator, not cruise). Per-agent personality: `speed_mult` (bits 24..34 of splitmix64 hash, range 0.85–1.20) and `pause_ms_for` (bits 40..52, range 200–400 ms) — disjoint from each other and from `stale_resume_gap_ms`/`personality_for`. Near desks arrive and sit before far desks (natural staggered arrivals). The *stateful* timeline that consumes these profiles lives in the sibling `motion` module (the render authority).
- "Which side does an agent approach furniture from?" → `layout::approach_point` (`layout/approach.rs`, this crate) is **A\*'s goal**; `stand_point` is the obstacle render anchor (they're equal for obstacles). A waypoint's `pos` is the furniture's blocked CENTER. Both pick the side **nearest the agent's home desk**, but `approach_point` adds two filters the old `walk_target` lacked: (1) the side must be in `FurnitureDef.approach` (`ApproachSides`, rotated by the seat's `facing` — a North-facing couch excludes its south backrest); (2) the chosen cell must be **A\*-reachable** (`ReachSet.reaches`, a coarse-cell BFS mirroring the router's grid coarsening) so A\* never targets a walled-off cell. Pure geometry over `WalkableMask` + `ReachSet`, no live A\*. Called by `pose::pure::idle_pose` (overlay walk dest) + `pixtuoid_scene::motion::pick_wander_dest` (routed walk dest) with the same `origin = home desk` so they can't drift.
  - **Obstacle kinds** (pantry/vending/printer): `approach_point == stand_point` — the agent stands AT the approach point, render anchor == A\* goal.
  - **Seat kinds** (`occupies_pos`: Couch/MeetingSofa/MeetingChair): A\* routes to the approach point (a cell off an *allowed* side), then a post-A\* **settle** appends `seated_foot_cell(furniture, pos)` (the seat's render-anchor *inverse* — `back_couch` seats → `pos+5`, waypoint stands → `pos`) to the walk polyline, so the sprite glides onto the seat **pop-free** (the settle endpoint == the render anchor by construction). The seat cell stays *blocked* (the sprite sits ON the furniture); the approach/settle split is what replaced the old "A\* snaps adjacent + sprite pops to a fixed north anchor." Don't collapse the settle back into a single `pos` target. **NO approach-side fallback:** if no allowed+reachable side exists (a seat boxed in to only its backrest — only happens in the degenerate tiny 120×96 meeting room), `approach_point` returns the blocked `pos` as a **"no valid approach" sentinel** and the caller (`pick_wander_dest`/`idle_pose`) **skips the furniture this cycle** (ambles aimlessly) rather than routing into it — a back-approach (standing behind the backrest then sitting facing north) is physically impossible, so it's never chosen. Invariant pinned by `seat_approach_is_never_behind_the_backrest_on_real_layouts`.
  - **The home desk** is a seat whose chair is `desk_walk_anchor_facing(desk, layout.desk_facing_at(desk))` — **which SIDE of the desk that lands on is a per-desk layout fact**, not a constant: a pod's two rows face each other, so row 0 seats its occupant on the FAR (north) side and row 1 on the NEAR (south) side. `layout.desk_facings` is the authority (parallel to `home_desks`, read via `desk_facing_at`/`desk_facing`); `seated_foot_cell(Desk, pos)` is deliberately facing-BLIND — it takes only a kind and a position, so it answers for the viewer-facing default and is NOT the chair for a back-turned desk. **Only the FAR chair sits in the desk's blocked BAND** — not in its footprint (the desk went walk-behind: the footprint is the shallow `DESK_FOOT_H` south strip, `ground_y: End`, and it is `mask.rs`'s `OBSTACLE_PAD_PX` routing pad that covers the chair row, with one pixel of slack) — so it can't be A\*'s goal directly: aiming the router at it makes `find_path` snap the blocked goal to the NEAREST walkable coarse cell, which for a far chair is the SOUTH/corridor front, so the agent routes up THROUGH the desk front (the "walk through the table" / approach-from-south bug). A NEAR chair sits past the desk's south base on OPEN floor, so that snap can't happen there and the indirection below is merely inert — don't read "the chair is blocked" as a desk invariant (it was asserted as one until the pod grew a second facing; `desk_approach_cell_is_never_inside_the_blocked_desk` now derives the pad rect from the same `furniture_def` row `mask::stamp_ground` reads, on both axes). **EVERY desk-touching leg — entry, wander-out, wander-back, and the exit DEPARTURE — therefore shares ONE `pose::desk_leg_endpoint(desk, layout)` helper** that returns `(routing_endpoint, chair_settle)`: the routing endpoint is `desk_approach_cell` (= `approach_point(Furniture::Desk, …, layout.desk_facing_at(desk))` — `DESK_APPROACH` is canonical facing-South and `ApproachSides::rotated` turns it, so a far-seated desk is approached from N/E/W with its south front excluded, and a back-turned one from its S/E/W with its NORTH side excluded; either way the excluded side is the one its own monitor blocks), and `chair_settle = Some(chair)` is the cell to SETTLE onto. So A\* always gets a real reachable goal and a post-A\* settle glides the sprite onto the chair; the chair stays the seat render anchor + the settle target, NEVER the raw router goal. `settle_seat_view` reads the desk's own facing to pick the arrival view — `SeatView::Back` for a back-turned chair, `Front` otherwise — and takes its z-key from the CHAIR (`cell.y`), so a far sitter keeps the historical `desk.y+4` key and sorts BEHIND the desk furniture (`desk.y+8`) while a near sitter's key lands below it and sorts IN FRONT. Matching on `seated_foot_cell`'s viewer-facing default instead simply never recognised a back-turned chair, and the settle silently lost its seat view. A wander leg uses `Settle::Both` (rise off one seat AND glide onto another — e.g. wander-back rises off the waypoint seat then glides onto the chair); entry/exit use single-ended `Settle::End`/`Settle::Start`; the motion-side wander/exit profiles add the chair-glide length so the duration matches (no pop). **Snap-back joins the unified path too** (the last exception erased): the urgent Idle→Active return routes via the approach cell + settle like the rest, run by **pure physics** with a brisk SnapBack profile — no 900 ms time-compression. Two things make that safe: (1) its arm gate measures distance to the **chair**, NOT the desk origin — the chair is +(5,4) from the origin for a far seat and +(5,12) for a near one, so a desk-origin gate re-fired forever once the agent settled ON the chair (9 px ≥ MIN); (2) it arms ONCE per state transition and renders from the FROZEN origin, so a K-call within a frame can't flip Walking↔Seated. The ONLY non-caller is a mid-wander EXIT, which starts from its live wander position rather than the chair. Degenerate fallback: if `desk_approach_cell` returns `None` (every allowed side walled off), the leg reverts to the direct chair target with no settle. So `approach_point(Furniture::Desk)` is called in production by every desk leg (entry/wander/exit/snap-back); seat render for the desk stays its own arms (4 work-states: typing/thinking/sleeping/`StandingAtDesk` at z=`desk.y+0`), NOT folded into `SeatView::seated_sprite` — only the arrival glide unifies. Pinned by `desk_entry_routes_around_the_desk_then_settles_onto_the_chair` + `wander_legs_approach_the_desk_via_an_allowed_side_not_through_the_front`. **Seating a desk EAST/WEST touches exactly three places**, because everything else is already a function of the facing rather than of north/south: (1) `desk_walk_anchor_facing` — the ONE site where `East`/`West` currently alias onto the viewer-facing offset (a deliberate render-a-slightly-wrong-office fallback, not a panic), and the offsets can't be symmetric because the sprite anchors at the FEET: a far seat clears the desk by 4 px while a near one needs 12; (2) a side-view desk sitter in `SeatView`/`seat_sprite`, alongside the `seated`/`seated_back` pair; (3) whichever placer emits the facing (`compute::pod_row_facing` today). The approach set rotates for all four already, `seated_anchor_facing` DERIVES from the walk anchor (so the no-arrival-pop identity holds for free), the walkable mask is facing-independent (a desk's blocked ground is its body), and `severed`'s south-fallback keeps degradation correct for any facing.
  - **Debug (debug builds only):** toggle `w` (`pixel_painter/debug_overlay.rs`) to see the mask (red), approach points (green) vs seat cells (magenta), and live A\* routes (cyan). The `w` dispatch arm and help entry are `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]`-gated — `w` does nothing in release builds. The snapshot example's `--debug-walkable` renders the same overlay + a BFS connectivity report. (The `w`-key dispatch arm lives in the painter `tui/mod.rs` event loop; the overlay it draws is here.)
- "How is the office rendered (pixel pass)?" → `pixtuoid_scene::pixel_painter::render_to_rgb_buffer`, internally `sim_step` (advance the world, no pixels — `pixel_painter/sim.rs`, see the layout tree above) then the paint pass over the resulting `&SimFrame`. The paint side is split: `pixel_painter/background/` (floor/walls/windows/clock/corridor/lighting/shadow via `mod.rs` + `sky.rs` + `lighting.rs`), `pixel_painter/drawable.rs` (y-sort `Drawable` enum + dispatch), `pixel_painter/effects.rs` (chair-behind/screen glow/sleep z/steam/dust/bubble), `pixel_painter/palette.rs` (agent palette + recolor + `tool_glow_tint` for per-tool monitor color), `pixel_painter/anchors.rs` (per-pose sprite anchors + breath + character_anchor; the walk lerp `walking_position` lives sim-side in `motion`), `pixel_painter/furniture.rs` (procedural furniture paint helpers). The terminal-flush pass (`half-block + widgets + status footer`) is the binary's `tui::renderer::draw_scene` (see [`../pixtuoid/src/tui/CLAUDE.md`](../pixtuoid/src/tui/CLAUDE.md)). **`floating::offscreen::OfficeRenderer`** (in the binary's `floating/` module, NOT here and NOT `tui/`) is the desktop window's render surface — it owns one `floor::FloorSession` (the scene-owned painter session over the shared `render_floor` seam above: sim stores + reusable buffer + coffee/chitchat + the dual eviction), returning a raw pixel buffer (NO half-block compression, NO ratatui `Terminal`). `floating::window` renders it at a DOWNSCALED size (~window/SCALE) then nearest-neighbor upscales, so the pixel-art stays chunky (a 1:1 blit renders 8×12 sprites at 8×12 px — unreadably tiny). It is NOT shared with `examples/snapshot.rs` (snapshot saves the ratatui `TestBackend` via `save_backend_as_png`, a ▀-compressed PNG with footer — a different output entirely). Unit-tested headlessly (`floating::offscreen::tests`); visual verification via `examples/floating_snapshot.rs` (renders the office through the same `OfficeRenderer` + scale to a PNG — the floating twin of the `snapshot` example).
- "How do agent name-badge labels work (the `cc·1a2b` text above each character)?" → the MODEL is `pixtuoid_scene::overlay::build_overlay` — one `LabelElement{anchor_px, text, tone, hovered}` per VISIBLE agent: `anchor_px` from `pixel_painter::character_anchor` (so the badge tracks the walking sprite, and off-floor agents — `character_anchor` returns `None` — are skipped, aligning labels 1:1 with painted sprites), `text` = the agent label, suffixed `·<id4>` (`disambig_suffix`) ONLY on a label collision with ≥4-char session id, then `truncate_label(_, DESK_W+4)`; `tone: LabelTone` = Exiting (if `exiting_at`) else Active/Waiting/Idle from `state`. It carries TONE not a resolved color, and since #657 every painter renders the SAME owner-ratified color split — the `●`/`▸` marker in the activity tone (`label_tone_rgb`), the NAME in the source's per-CLI badge hue (`theme.source.by_prefix` on the label's pre-`·` prefix — the same hue the dashboard/Sources/tooltip badges use; whole label falls back to tone on an unregistered prefix, hover overrides to all-White BOLD). The status-dot idiom: dot = busy/idle, text = identity. Per-surface: **tui** `widgets/tooltip.rs::paint_label_widgets` → a two-`Span` ratatui `Line` (px→cell), guarded by `tui_renderer/harness` label tests; **floating** `floating::offscreen::paint_labels_into_surface` → two AA text runs (marker then name, x-advanced by the marker's measured width) in **Monaspace Neon** (binary `crate::aa_text`, `ab_glyph`) blitted at NATIVE surface res POST-upscale, `anchor_px × scale` centered over the sprite, a near-black drop-shadow for legibility over the office (floating-only — the tui has cell backgrounds). Same model, divergent painters = the "unify the UI, per-target impl" split. Visual: `examples/floating_snapshot.rs --agents N`. The THIRD painter is the site: the wasm canvas upscales a small buffer with `image-rendering: pixelated`, so baked AA text would blow up blocky — instead `pixtuoid-web`'s `Office::overlay_json` exports this same model + the board as JSON, and `site` lays crisp Monaspace Neon DOM spans over the canvas (see [`../../site/CLAUDE.md`](../../site/CLAUDE.md)).
- "How do the room dividers render (frosted-glass partitions)?" → the wall's RENDER half lives in **`pixel_painter/wall.rs`** (paint + `enqueue_room_walls_h/v`, the y-sort emitters `render_to_rgb_buffer` calls); the GEOMETRY half (thickness consts, `WallDef`/`wall_segment_rect`, `stitch_vertical_wall`) lives one layer down in **`layout::rooms::walls`**, and the two are bound by shared consts + the stitch fn. The glass is painted AFTER `dim_floor_overlay` so it isn't dimmed. Each layout segment is a frosted-glass strip: a cool gradient across the short axis (a bright specular edge → tinted body → soft slate edge, all three derived from `room_wall_trim_light` and alpha-composited via `glass_over` (`palette::blend`) so the floor glows through), a subtle brighter **seam** every `GLASS_SEAM_STRIDE` px, a darker **mullion post** every `MULLION_STRIDE=10` px (interior-only — an end post would double the door jambs; the two rhythms interleave), and — as of #559 — **dark door jambs** (`DOOR_JAMB_PX=2`, `room_wall_trim_dark`) flanking every opening. The jambs draw from **`SceneLayout.doorways`** (the wall resolver HANDS its cut openings to the renderer — `rooms/walls.rs` is the one place that knows every door; the painter never re-infers gaps from segment adjacency). BOTH orientations' jambs now ride their y-sorted drawable via cut-end flags computed at enqueue (the paint pass has no layout access): horizontal via `jamb_left`/`jamb_right` on `RoomWallH` (`paint_door_jamb_h`), vertical via `jamb_north`/`jamb_south` on `RoomWallV` (`paint_door_jamb_v`, posts COVER the endpoint-inclusive cut ends — a review-caught off-by-one class). The vertical's joint stitching is the pure `stitch_vertical_wall` helper — in `layout::rooms::walls` (the wall's geometry home) and SHARED by the walkable-mask footprint (`wall_segment_rect`, same module) AND the painter's `enqueue_room_walls_v` (`pixel_painter/wall.rs`), so the blocked ground and the drawn glass meet the band/crossing walls identically (no drift, no corner hole). Thicknesses: **`WALL_THICK_H_PX=6` (E-W, shows its face) vs `WALL_THICK_V_PX=4` (N-S, edge-on; was 3 — it read as a render artifact at doll-house sizes, #559)**. BOTH `WALL_THICK_H_PX`/`WALL_THICK_V_PX` are **defined as `layout::WALL_THICK_H`/`WALL_THICK_V`** (the mask footprints) — one source of truth per axis, so the visible face and the blocked ground can't drift (the vertical pair silently diverged at #559 when the mask stayed a 1px line + isotropic pad while the glass drew 4px — the mask drifted W of the glass AND left the east glass column walkable). The vertical wall's blocked footprint is `visual` MINUS a north walk-behind cap (`WALL_TOP_OVERHANG_PX`, sized to the E-W `GLASS_CAP_PX`) at a FREE terminus (a run below a door), so a character parked behind its top reads *behind* the glass — the desk's walk-behind shape on the shared `ground_rect` knob, now that the V wall joins the y-sort. A JOINTED terminus (`y_top != seg_top` — the window band OR a crossing horizontal wall, anything `stitch` raised) drops the cap to full `footprint == visual` coverage: the joint has a wall/band above it, so a cap there only opens a walkable HOLE between the two walls' footprints (a bite out of the divider). A short run below a door keeps ≥`WALL_THICK_V` rows blocked (the `min` guard) so it never becomes a second opening. **Paint order / occlusion:** BOTH orientations are now y-sorted drawables (`DrawableKind::RoomWallH`/`RoomWallV`, `paint_glass_wall_h`/`_v`), anchored at their **south (front) base** — so a character standing just north of either is composited *behind* the glass (occluded), same depth trick the `Door` drawable uses. The V wall's z-key is its RAW south end, NOT the corner-extended `y_bot` (`stitch`'s L-notch fill), so at an inside corner the crossing H wall still paints over the V (`enqueue_room_walls_v` emits V before H so that tie stays H-on-top). To make that occlusion visible in a pure top-down projection, the horizontal glass rises `GLASS_CAP_PX=6` px NORTH of its footprint (a translucent "back cap" over the meeting-room floor — visual only, not in the mask; `GLASS_CAP_PX` is defined as `WALL_THICK_H_PX` so the cap grows with the wall-face thickness — was 3, which only grazed the single feet row), so a walker's feet/legs read behind the frosted pane. The loop also **stitches the vertical's joints** the terminal-agnostic layout can't know about: a segment starting at `top_margin` is raised to `top_wall_h` (connect to the window band), a segment sitting just below a horizontal wall is bridged up to it (the dual-meeting layout offsets the lower segment ~6 px to clear the cross wall), and a segment whose bottom meets a horizontal row extends down by the horizontal's thickness to fill the inside corner (the mask stamps the SAME extended rect — one shared stitch — so the blocked footprint has no corner hole either). Covered by `harness::meeting_glass_partition_connects_at_window_and_corner` + `pixel_painter::tests::glass_wall_h_back_cap_composites_over_a_character_behind_it` / `glass_wall_v_composites_over_a_character_behind_its_north_cap` + `layout::rooms::walls::tests::vertical_wall_below_a_crossing_wall_drops_its_north_cap` + `layout::placement_sweep::no_walkable_hole_where_a_vertical_wall_meets_a_horizontal_one`.
- "How does the neon wall board work?" → `pixel_painter/background/lighting.rs::paint_neon_panel` paints the dark panel with pulsing cyan border in the pixel buffer; the 3-row ratatui text overlay (brand + ★ CTA, the plain-English mood pulse, office context) is the binary's `tui::widgets::wall_board.rs::paint_wall_display` (see [`../pixtuoid/src/tui/CLAUDE.md`](../pixtuoid/src/tui/CLAUDE.md)). The board text pins to the panel's dark INTERIOR — this crate exposes `pub const NEON_PANEL_INNER_X`/`NEON_PANEL_INNER_W` (the outer `NEON_PANEL_W` minus its 1px frame on each side, `NEON_PANEL_BORDER`), and the binary's board derives its cell-origin + width from them so the lit text sits inside the glowing frame (laying it to the full outer width overran it). `paint_neon_panel` reads the SAME `NEON_PANEL_BORDER`, so the dark cells it leaves provably == the cells the text may fill; a compile-time assert pins interior < outer. - "How does weather work?" → `pixel_painter/background/sky.rs::weather_state` picks from 8 variants (Clear/Rain/Storm/Snow/Fog/Overcast/Windy/Smog) via splitmix64 hash of `wallclock / 600` (changes every 10 min) — unchanged by the sun/moon rework. Weather is now an ATMOSPHERE, not a light level: **`sky::atmo(weather) -> Atmo { direct, diffuse, disc }`** is a pure transmission model (each channel 0..1) — `direct` scales the hard directional beam (wall sun-spot + dust motes, via `beam_strength`), `diffuse` is the flat ambient fill, `disc` is how much of the sun/moon body itself shows through the medium. Overcast/Rain/Storm all sit at the same near-zero `disc` (thick cloud hides the body uniformly — a thicker cloud never shows MORE of it than a thinner one), and **Storm now transmits LESS than Rain** on both `direct` and `diffuse` (the physics was corrected — cumulonimbus is optically denser than nimbostratus; the old model had this backwards). The actual LIGHT comes from `sky::emitter(now)` (the sun/moon — see the entry below), and `time_of_day_look` folds `emitter_lum × atmo` into one interior illuminance, so brightness is altitude-coupled (a stormy noon out-lights a stormy dusk) instead of each weather carrying its own absolute light level. `background::skyline_haze(weather)` murks the city behind the glass under fog/storm/rain/smog (fog is a near-total white-out) — it and the Fog/Overcast/Smog on-glass washes are VEILS, scaled to the emitter by `veil_lum`/`veil_lit` so they darken after dusk (see the sharp edge). City light twinkle is ~0.6–1.4s cycles at 75% lit. - "How does the sun/moon sky-light work?" → `pixel_painter/background/sky.rs` owns the unified model. `sky::emitter(now) -> SkyState { body, altitude, azimuth, warmth, emitter_lum }` is the ONE physical light source — sun by day, moon by night, riding a sine arc: `altitude` (0 horizon..1 apex) drives intensity, `azimuth` (0 east/dawn..1 west/dusk) drives position, `warmth` runs high at the horizon and neutral at apex, and `emitter_lum` is the luminance reaching the atmosphere (for the moon, scaled by `moon_phase(now)` — a synodic-month illuminated fraction, so a full moon lights brighter than a new moon). `sky::atmo(weather)` (see the weather entry above) is the transmission the emitter's light passes through. `time_of_day_look`, `beam_strength`, and `sun_on_wall` all DERIVE from this one `SkyState` + `Atmo` pair — sharing a single `azimuth`/`altitude` is why the disc's position, the floor-spill lean, and the wall sun-spot can never disagree: `time_of_day_look` folds `emitter_lum × atmo` into glass tint + spill strength/slant + `darkness`; `sun_on_wall` projects the SAME azimuth onto the office's East/South/West walls (`None` at night — the moon casts no wall spot); `beam_strength` is `emitter_lum × atmo(weather).direct`, gated to the sun only (zero at night — the moon casts no usable direct beam). The disc's PLACEMENT + the night-star helpers live in `background/celestial.rs` (#469): `compute_disc` (placement + per-theme `sun_core`/`moon_core` color, `None` under thick cloud), `night_star_strength`/`star_exists`/`star_twinkle`, and `golden_hour_blaze`. They feed the sky branch of `paint_floor_to_ceiling_window` in `background/mod.rs`, which PAINTS the disc core/glow plus the moon's crescent/gibbous terminator (`lit_frac = moon_phase(now)`) and the star field.

- "What is the elastic wander timeline?" → `advance_wander()` in `motion/mod.rs` drives the cyclic wander via explicit per-phase clocks anchored to `wander.phase_started_at`. Walk legs (WalkingOut/WalkingBack) use `physics::walk_profile` — variable duration proportional to path length; Seated/AtWaypoint dwell is **absolute per-spot**: `seated_dwell_ms(id)` for the desk beat (15-30s) and `dwell_ms(kind, id)` for the waypoint beat (sofa/meeting seat 20-40s, pantry 10-18s, vending/printer 4-8s). The same `dwell_ms`/`seated_dwell_ms` are read by the stateless `idle_pose` (pose/pure.rs) for overlay coherence, BUT `idle_pose`'s own cycle period uses fixed estimates (`WANDER_WALK_EST_MS`/`WANDER_DWELL_EST_MS`/`est_wander_cycle_ms`) since exact coherence is impossible (the pure half has no router). Total cycle wall-time is elastic — it grows with actual path length — but `wander.cycle_n` is deterministic so destination selection is unchanged from the stateless wander. **Idempotency**: transitions fire only when `now > wander.last_advanced_at`. **Bootstrap**: fresh Idle agents (UNIX_EPOCH sentinel on `wander.phase_started_at`) fast-forward `cycle_n = elapsed_idle / est_wander_cycle_ms(id)`; the phase clock anchors at `now` (clean Seated start) so the machine never rushes through expired legs (a desk↔waypoint teleport). **Stale resume**: the TRIGGER is `now - wander.last_advanced_at > stale_resume_gap_ms(id)` (7-13s — a frame-cadence-vs-frozen-floor sentinel, NOT a dwell detector: on-screen `advance_wander` runs every ~33ms even during a 40s lounge dwell, so the gap never approaches it; only an off-screen floor or a pause trips it). On trip it re-runs the same analytic bootstrap instead of replaying the whole backlog one transition per frame. Do NOT raise the trigger to "max dwell" — that would let 13-60s off-screen gaps replay. **Seated render authority**: in `pixtuoid_scene::pose::derive_with_routing` the `WanderPhase::Seated` arm returns `Pose::SeatedIdle` DIRECTLY — it must NOT fall through to the stateless `idle_pose` (pose/pure.rs), whose independent fixed-fraction timeline drifts from this machine and would render the agent at a waypoint while the scene clock says Seated (a teleport). Don't "simplify" the Seated arm back to a fallthrough.

- "How does the gateway mascot (the OpenClaw lobster) work?" → daemon presences are GLOBAL, not per-desk: `floor::project_floor_scene` carries `SceneState`'s WHOLE daemon roster onto the GROUND floor only, so each mascot renders exactly once (pinned by `daemons_projects_onto_the_ground_floor_only`, which stages TWO gateways). Movement lives in `creatures.rs` (extracted from `pixel_painter/drawable.rs` in #660): `walkable_target` draws each destination from the whole walkable floor; `mascot_wander`/`mascot_home`/`mascot_elevator` drive it. **ONE mascot per daemon INSTANCE, not per source**: `enqueue_gateway_mascots` (plural — it returns a frame per drawn mascot) iterates `(source, instance, presence)` — the SPRITE + display name still resolve from the source (`gateway_mascot_def`), while the wander seed folds `source` AND `instance.as_str()` (`mascot_seed`), so two OpenClaw gateways take different paths instead of stacking on one cell (a source-only seed rendered two gateways as one lobster). `PixelPassResult::mascots` is therefore a `Vec<MascotFrame>` — each frame carries its own `instance` so the binary's hover names WHICH gateway (`OpenClaw:19789`), set only when THAT SOURCE has a sibling instance (per-source, not roster-wide — a future second daemon source with one gateway each already reads apart by name and sprite), so the single-gateway tooltip stays byte-unchanged. Pinned through the rendered hover text by `the_port_suffix_names_a_gateway_only_when_it_has_a_sibling` (the painter's decision is invisible to any other assertion). The walk-IN is staggered per instance (`MASCOT_ENTER_STAGGER_MS`): the seed reaches only the steady wander, so two gateways whose presence first-sights in the same beat used to lerp the identical elevator→home line and render as ONE lobster for the full 2.2s — the common case when pixtuoid starts while both gateways are already up. The walk-in is staggered per instance (`MASCOT_ENTER_STAGGER_MS`) and an instance is simply NOT DRAWN until its own delay elapses — so two gateways first-sighted in one beat cannot lerp the identical elevator→home line, and superposition at the door is unrepresentable rather than merely avoided (it used to need a per-instance door offset to undo).
>
> **Destinations come from the WHOLE walkable floor (`walkable_target`), for BOTH creatures and every daemon state — and that replaced a curated per-furniture list because the list WAS the crowding.** It held only 3-25 points depending on terminal size (a 100x30 window has 2 desks), and the idle set was structurally 5 however big the office got, so N creatures shared destinations by pigeonhole. Every earlier fix was a bolt-on around that: per-state spot sets, a seeded standing-offset ring with boxed-in and crowded-spot fallbacks, an elevator hold needing its own offset. Measured through `mascot_position` (production layout, 1080 frames, four idle gateways on consecutive ports): **85% of frames crowded under the list, 42% drawing from the floor**, and the refactor deleted ~370 lines of code and tests. Both figures are BOX overlap (14x12 boxes intersecting), the PESSIMISTIC metric — a lobster does not fill its box, so at N=2 box overlap ran 37% while contiguous red pixels merged in 5.5%. Pinned by `four_gateways_rarely_crowd_now_that_the_whole_floor_is_in_play`, whose bound catches a return to a small set (a forced 5-cell set measures 99%).
>
> Two things stay true and are worth not re-discovering. (a) The residue is mostly two mascots WALKING: 45% of every cycle is a routed leg and legs share the office's aisles however far apart their endpoints are, so no destination rule drives crowding to zero. (b) Expanding each anchor into its 8 neighbours instead of spreading was measured WORSE than the list at small sizes (62% -> 74%): 8 cells within 4px are ONE place to a 14x12 sprite. Spread, not offset, is the mechanism.
>
> Destinations come from the whole mask, but the RESOLVED point is clamped by the sprite's own
> extent (`creatures::clamp_sprite_inside`, applied at every `mascot_position`/`pet_position` exit):
> `blit_centered` parks a frame at `pos - size/2` and CLIPS at the buffer edge, so an unclamped
> east draw rendered half a lobster pinned to the border (measured 3 of 8 independent port sets at
> 192x80). Clamping the resolved POINT rather than insetting the destination draw is deliberate —
> it leaves every seeded destination exactly where it was, so only the frames that would have
> spilled move. The PET rides the same rule (same `walkable_target`, same centred blit).
>
> What was GIVEN UP, deliberately: destinations no longer encode daemon state (state reads from the CADENCE — 4.5s busy / 9s idle / 14s degraded — and the sprite tint), and a creature may rest in open floor rather than beside furniture. Don't re-add claim/probe machinery to tighten this: that would couple one mascot's motion to which siblings exist, breaking the stateless invariant the whole module is built on.
- "How does the lofi soundtrack get composed and rendered (and how do I add an instrument / a stem)?" → `audio/compose.rs::compose(mood, seed)` samples a theory-constrained grammar (10 day + 8 night progression templates × transpose, melody/groove/keys/bass rules) into a `GeneratedScore`; `audio/synth.rs::gen_beds` renders its SIX lanes (`bank::TRACK_STEMS` order: pad, sparkle, keys, drums, texture, bass) through the same cores the frozen `#[cfg(test)]` fingerprint anchors pin. The lead INSTRUMENT registry is `compose::LeadVoice` (EpVel + Pluck + Kalimba day-pool, EpVel + Vibraphone night-pool) dispatched once in `synth::lead_voice_fn`; the voice draw sits LAST in the seed stream. A new instrument = the 5-step checklist in `.claude/skills/procedural-lofi` (voice fn → variant → draw weight → distribution test → `examples/lofi_audition` listen batch, `--solo <lane>` isolates a lane). A new STEM is an arc, not a checklist: `LoopStem` (+ keep Rain LAST), `TRACK_STEMS`, `StemLevels` + its tier-gain table, `lane_recipe` + `gen_bed` arm (mind the silent `_ => Vec::new()` fallback — see the sharp edge), the generated-beds length/band asserts, the audition mix arrays, and the wasm/site gain-node counts (`pixtuoid-web/src/audio.rs` warmup tests, `OfficeBackdrop.astro` loops). Quality gate is STATISTICAL: blind-audition a seed batch, ratify the GENERATOR.
