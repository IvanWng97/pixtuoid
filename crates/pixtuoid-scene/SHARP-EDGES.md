# pixtuoid-scene — known sharp edges

Indexed one line each in [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md). These look like bugs and are deliberate design — read the entry before "fixing" one. An entry states the edge, the WHY, and where the authority lives (the pinning test, the in-code comment, or the issue); the full adjudication history is in the cited issue/PR, not here.

- **Agent OUTFIT (shirt+pants) is keyed on the normalized `cwd`, not `agent_id`** (Team Palette): same working directory → same outfit, so the office reads as a color-coded org-chart; hair/skin stay `agent_id`-seeded for distinctness, and empty/unknown cwd falls back to the id seed. The `examples/snapshot` fixture assigns VARIED cwds so the gallery shows grouping — don't collapse it to one cwd.

- **`recolor_frame` substitutes by RGB equality**, which works because each recolor key maps to a unique RGB — enforced at pack load (`validate_recolor_palette` bails on a collision, embedded and `--pack-dir` alike). The key set is `pixtuoid_core::sprite::format::RECOLOR_KEYS`, the single source both the substitution and the guard iterate. If two keys ever need to share a color, switch to palette-key indexing instead.

- **EXIT walks are time-compressed to fit the GC window; entry/wander/snap-back are not.** An exiting slot races `EXIT_GRACE_WINDOW` (4.5s), so `derive_with_routing` scales `elapsed` to arrive by the deadline — delete the compression and the sprite vanishes mid-corridor (a fixed regression). Snap-back runs pure physics with a brisk profile (`SNAP_BACK_MS` is only the ARM window now, not a render cap); entry has no cap and must stay uncompressed. **A CANCELLED walkout re-enters through the door once ARRIVED, and from its live position while IN-FLIGHT** — `resurrect_in_place` can't re-arm the spawn gate (`created_at` isn't re-stamped) and the snap-back is `Active`/`Waiting`-only, so the entry branch re-arms the leg itself (that ordering is the seam; an earlier claim that snap-back covered it measured as a 67–126px teleport). The `take()` of the spent exit leg is load-bearing: a retained arrived leg replays on the NEXT exit and the sprite vanishes on its first frame.

- **A walk leg's A\* polyline shape is frozen once per leg, not re-routed per frame.** The router's own cache is invalidated by per-frame occupancy churn; without the freeze a mid-leg re-route remaps frozen progress onto a different polyline — the sprite jumps and the frame pays fresh A\* (the "flash" + stutter). Only cornered routes (>2 points) freeze; straight walks re-route each frame (cheap, self-healing). Accepted: a frozen walker won't dodge a mid-leg obstruction (rare, cosmetic). Don't "simplify" the `walk_path` check away as duplicate caching.

- **A meeting room narrower than `MEETING_FURNITURE_MIN_W` has NO sofa/table/seats — bare floor, BY DESIGN.** Below it the coarse router can't reach the seats, so a seated trip would teleport (find_path None → straight-line fallback); the room shell stays, only unroutable furniture drops. Guarded by `pathfind::tests::every_wander_waypoint_is_routable_on_the_coarse_grid`.

- **Occlusion is EMERGENT — there is no `occludes_behind` field / synthetic cap (deleted).** A piece's blocked rect is its `footprint` offset inside the `visual` box by declared `ground_x`/`ground_y` (`GroundAlign`), all derived through ONE shared ground-geometry formula (`mask::ground_rect`); an overhanging sprite (plant canopy, TV, whiteboard panel, pantry counter's shallow south strip, the desk's 2px `DESK_FOOT_H` base) occludes a walker parked behind it via its own y-sort — the industry base-collision + sprite-occludes pattern. Note `Center` is center-ON-pos (`v/2 − f/2`), not center-in-box — they differ by 1px at opposite parity (the floor lamp exposed it). The MASK uses the shallow footprint; `approach_point` derives the user's park distance from the full `visual` (`approach_clearance_extent`, the single extent source) so a user parks clear of the sprite. **Don't re-add a cap** — make the sprite overhang its footprint instead. Pinned by `every_pod_occludes_via_overhang`.

- **splitmix64 is open-coded FOUR times in this crate on purpose** (sky weather, lightning, dust motes, outfit seed) beside the canonical `core::id::splitmix64`, plus a separate family of simpler single-multiply position/twinkle mixes — each an independent noise source over a disjoint input domain, so no two sites need equal output and no parity test is wanted; the real gate is the gen-check pixel diff. Don't unify them into one shared fn.

- **`paint_glass_wall_h` and `paint_glass_wall_v` stay SEPARATE — don't hoist a `paint_glass_strip(axis, alphas)` helper.** They share only the 5-branch tone-ladder SKELETON and diverge at every load-bearing point (cap vs no cap, seam axis, per-orientation alphas — a face-on wall reads at a different opacity than an edge-on one); the unifier's interface would be as wide as the body it hides. Adjudicated against in the architecture-deepening sweep; revisit only if a THIRD wall orientation appears.

- **`epoch_ms(now)` is the ABSOLUTE wall-clock ms (~1.7e12) — reduce it BEFORE any `as f32`.** At that magnitude an f32 ULP is ~131s, so a direct cast freezes any animation reading it (the neon-pulse/dust-mote freeze). Reduce to a bounded phase with an INTEGER op first (`% CYCLE_MS`), or do the math in f64 and cast the final value. A pinning test MUST use a wall-clock-scale `now`, not a small test epoch, or it never sees the freeze.

- **The sun/moon disc keeps a "real low window":** visible only at low altitude, clipped above the glass near apex, gated to the ONE window its centre is over (hides behind the pillar/elevator), and hidden uniformly under thick cloud (`MIN_DISC_VIS`). All intended day/night + weather gating (`background/celestial.rs`).

- **Day-over-night light invariant:** the moon casts no direct beam, and `solar_noon_outshines_the_brightest_night` guards the calibration — don't retune moon luminance, `city_bounce`, or a weather's `atmo` without re-running it. That guard reads `darkness` (interior illuminance) and is structurally BLIND to anything painted on the glass afterwards — a foggy midnight pane once out-shone a stormy noon pane while `darkness` stayed correct — so pane-side pins assert on the RENDERED buffer as a CONTRAST FLOOR (`MAX_NIGHT_PANE_FRACTION`), not a bare ordering (an ordering-only assertion passed on the defective painter). See `background/tests.rs`.

- **The weather VEILS are lit by the emitter, and cross-weather brightness ordering is NOT an invariant.** Veils scale with `veil_lum` (emitter's own luminance + `NIGHT_VEIL_FLOOR` city scatter — folding in `atmo`/`darkness` would darken a stormy noon twice). A lit white-out fog genuinely out-shines clear sky, and low cloud genuinely glows at night (`fog_still_glows_over_the_midnight_sky` is the counter-pin); "heavier weather is never brighter" would assert taste, not physics.

- **An EXCLUSIVE spot is single-occupancy, enforced where the destination is CHOSEN — not where it's drawn.** `SpotClaims` + a forward linear-probe resolve contention (venue seats are contiguous in `layout.waypoints`, so the probe tries a sibling seat first). The gate is `furniture_def(..).exclusive`, never a hand-listed kind set (`no_exclusive_waypoint_kind_ever_steps_aside` ranges `WaypointKind::ALL`); `exclusive` (capacity) is a separate field from `occupies_pos` (render/approach) — they diverge for the stand-beside singles, and `occupies_pos ⇒ exclusive` is pinned by `furniture_def_invariants_hold_for_every_row`. Two gates are load-bearing: the claim needs `phase != Seated` (the phase is the honest "actually out there" signal), and `derive_with_routing` RELEASES on the non-wander path (else an Active burst freezes a mid-trip target and leaks the spot unboundedly). `waypoint_rank_offset_x` returns 0 for every exclusive spot — its `STEP_ASIDE_DX` queue offset is a pre-per-seat-waypoint fossil that parked a second sitter ON the meeting table. Don't re-add render-time seat de-collision: an exact overlap is the honest render of a claim bug.

- **The free-standing whiteboard OVERLAPS a north-facing desk's seat in the committed stills — an ACCEPTED look, not a bug.** The board's aisle band IS the north pod rows' seat band, the ground strip clears the seat cell (routing unaffected), and the owner reviewed the crop and accepted it. Don't nudge the board's x without asking — the aisle is the only band `snap_inter_pod_ground_y` may use, and moving out gives up the desk capacity the tightening bought. `gen-check` diffs against these same committed bytes and cannot see it either way.

- **`desk_facings` is index-parallel to `home_desks` and stays that way — consolidation into one `Vec<Desk>` was weighed and declined.** Both are `pub` on a published crate (a second breaking change later), the read side is already funnelled through `desk_facing`/`desk_facing_at`, and both vectors come from ONE `compute_pod_desks` call — not the multi-site hand-sync the correlated-state factor targets. Guards: the `debug_assert_eq!` inside `desk_facing` + `placement_sweep::every_desk_has_a_facing`. If ever consolidated, do it while the minor is unreleased.

- **A back-turned desk with no reachable south approach is DEMOTED to viewer-facing — and that rung never fires in any swept layout.** It is a net for a future placer, not live code (0 demotions across the sweep), so a pod whose rows face the same way is a `pod_row_facing` bug, not this. Deleting it is still wrong: the probe makes the claim measurable, and `assert_home_desk_approaches_are_routable` panics on a stranded desk rather than silently seating one off its routed side.

- **Two narrow-band connectivity guards keep the office ONE region (#566), both graceful DECOR degradation — not bugs.** At specific narrow bands the lounge couch can seal the door column (gated off via `couch_clears_door` — why `door` is computed above the lounge gate) and a relocated scatter plant can plug the sole inter-pod drain (post-mask flood-fill from `door_threshold` drops aisle-resident plants, rebuild once). **Do not expect a per-rung mutation to red**: the rungs are a redundancy LADDER on one predicate — disabling one just escalates to the next; only removing the WHOLE branch reds the connectivity tests, which is the negative control to use. The connectivity PREDICATE is the ROUTER's, not a pixel flood's: a ≤3px channel is pixel-connected but coarse-impassable, so `severed` fires when EITHER a pixel pocket survives OR any home desk loses its reachable `approach_point`. The narrow widths/heights live in `narrow_band_connectivity_boundary_scan` + `SWEEP_SIZES` — the discrete sweep grid had structurally skipped them (#566's lesson: sweep axes must cover the failure's own resolution). The spawn point is pinned SEPARATELY (`the_spawn_threshold_stands_on_the_floor_not_the_wall_apron`) because routing guards snap a displaced seed back into the component and stay green on a spawn inside the wall band.

- **Both layout floors are DERIVED per axis and deliberately UNIFORM across variants — a narrow-left-column floor that could seat desks below them is refused anyway.** `MIN_LAYOUT_W`/`_H` solve `band_w`/`band_h` against `DESK_BAND_MIN_W`/`_H` (the two terms `compute_pod_desks`' clamp compares on each axis) using the WIDEST variant column, so widths 32-36 refuse even on OpenPlan/Dense/Lounge, which did seat desks there. That is the price of one number the painter can say out loud: the too-small notice is a promise about the TERMINAL, and a per-variant gate would flip office↔notice as the user changes floors, at a size where a refusal is already indistinguishable from a crash. Pinned by `every_floor_variant_seats_a_desk_at_the_minimum_layout_size` (+ the `sweep_over` acceptance arm); the placer-side boundary by `min_band_w_is_the_placers_own_first_desk_boundary`, both axes.

- **The size gate cannot be replaced by a search over the real placer — below it `compute_with_seed` is out of contract, and RELEASE fails silently.** The early `None` is also the precondition guard for the whole body: below the floor the `#566` guard's terminal `debug_assert!` fires on some seeds (a pocket survives dropping every scatter plant AND the whiteboard), and under `buf_h < top_margin` the `usable_h = buf_h - top_margin` subtraction underflows two lines in. Both are DEBUG-only (release sets neither `debug-assertions` nor `overflow-checks`) — measured, a release build panics ZERO times across the sub-floor band: it returns a desk-less layout, some carrying a sealed walkable pocket, and below `top_margin` a zero-height band. Failing silently is worse than crashing, not better. So `min_layout_size()` derives its answer from the same band formulas the placer uses rather than probing the placer itself, pinned to the placer by `min_band_w_is_the_placers_own_first_desk_boundary` (both axes).

- **The free-standing whiteboard stands ONLY in an inter-pod aisle, and is ABSENT rather than relocated when the band holds a single pod row.** Its `usable_h / 3` anchor is a hint, not a slot — unsnapped it sealed the west lane, and `free_standing_whiteboard_survives_the_west_aisle_it_used_to_seal` (placement_sweep) now asserts it SURVIVING via `snap_inter_pod_ground_y`. The bands are deliberately NOT "every strip no pod occupies": the north margin and south remainder are already spoken for (lounge, door approach) — don't restore the short-floor board by widening the band set. A pod's extent prices `DESK_GROUND_H`, not the slot pitch, because blocked ground runs past the last slot row.

- **A desk chair's z key TIES with its occupant's and the order is decided by INSERTION, not by the key.** `enqueue_desk_chairs` runs after `enqueue_characters` and the sort is stable — reorder the calls or swap in an unstable sort and the chair paints UNDER the sitter with every test green. The tie is structural (both read the same seat cell's row), so it can't drift apart; `both_profiles_seat_an_occupant_on_the_side_the_layout_chose` pins the facing side, and the `const _` assert beside `DESK_WALK_Y_OFF_BACK` guards the one edit (`< WALKING_Y_OFF`) that could clamp a sprite off its chair. A position sweep is vacuous here — an earlier revision shipped one, green with the offset zeroed.

- **Every wander destination is filtered by `ReachSet`, but the ±4px `jitter_dest` perturbation applies AFTER that filter — an accepted residual.** The jitter is a lockstep contract (rendered shape, profile length, and router-cache key all read the same jittered goal), so a dest-side re-filter must move all three together. Don't clamp inside `jitter_dest` alone.

- **The GENERATED night pad renders NO sub-bass — the BASS stem owns that register; only the frozen v4 anchor still bakes its sub in.** Re-adding a baked sub doubles the low end AND (because `night_pad_core` peak-normalizes) pushes the chords back out of the freed headroom — coupled effects, not tunable alone; deleting the anchor's sub invalidates the v4 fingerprint pins. Related traps: `gen_bed`'s `_ => Vec::new()` arm means a lane wired into `TRACK_STEMS` without a match arm ships SILENT with no compile error (the per-lane asserts in `generated_beds_are_finite_phase_locked_and_in_the_sound_world` are the tooth — add the lane there in the same change), and `LoopStem::Rain` must stay LAST in `ALL` (the wasm handoff slots track beds by index with rain at `TRACK_STEMS.len()`).

- **Every ARTIFICIAL FLOOR light scales by `indoor_scale`; the self-lit WALL fixtures deliberately do not.** An emptied floor reads dark because its lights go out, not via a second darkening (the old `EMPTY_FLOOR_DIM_BOOST` was deleted for faking exactly that). Scaled: ceiling pools, floor-lamp halo, both per-desk emitters. Unscaled: occupant-gated glows (already gated), neon panel + clock (self-lit), daylight (sun enters an empty office). A FIFTH floor emitter added without the factor is invisible to the suite — extend the set at `desk_light` and pin it in `an_emptied_floor_takes_both_desk_emitters_down_with_the_level`.

- **The hour's cast on objects runs as TWO `wash_since` passes over a snapshot diff, and foreground EMITTERS are deliberately inside the second one.** The grouping axis is PAINT ORDER; the two passes' exclusions differ on purpose (emitters + self-lit fixtures painted before the first snapshot escape; `paint_shadow`/`paint_ambient` already take `look`, folding them in would apply the hour twice). The desk lamp's halo IS washed while the floor lamp's is not — measured and accepted (the wash costs ~a fifth of the lamp's lift with the peak intact; the exemption alternative is a coverage set threaded through three signatures with a silent failure mode). Known blind spots of the snapshot diff (identical-color pixels, alpha-blended veils double-washing the floor beneath) are the same priced trade. Don't move the desk lamp above the snapshot: it would paint under a seated occupant.

- **The north wall band is SOLID for its whole width — the elevator doorway is a hole in the WALL, not in the ground — and a sprite overlapping the band is the intended look.** `build_walkable_mask` blocks the full band and takes no door input; every entry/exit leg starts at `door_threshold`, which stands clear of it. Re-cutting a channel (#902) is invisible to every routing guard, and the golden masks move WITH a cut rather than finding one (deleting the cut reds only `walkable_golden` — the negative control); the tooth is `placement_sweep::no_cell_of_the_north_wall_band_is_walkable`, because `creatures::walkable_target` rejection-samples destinations straight off the mask — a cut cell is a legal draw, and a cat walked up the channel. A sprite overlapping the band is NOT a defect: sprites are feet-anchored (invariant #6), only the CENTRE has to be real floor, and `walkable_target` separately requires `is_visually_clear` on the snapped candidate.

- **Layout variety is spent out of budgets that already exist — a scattered plant can only stand where a DISPLACED one already could.** The scatter changes only which step of `settle_plant`'s existing inward ladder is tried first; a free ±N two-axis nudge leaves the container (`every_piece_ground_stays_in_its_container` catches it). Candidates settle FOLDED against ones already placed. `compute_pod_decor` picks from a SHUFFLED BAG per pass with a rotate-on-collision walk between passes — don't collapse the walk into a single shuffle (the pass SEAM is what the adjacency rule is about), and don't swap the bag for a seeded rotation (one slot's kind would fix every later one). Pinned by `a_plants_spot_varies_by_floor`, `every_pod_decor_kind_can_open_a_floor`, `no_two_adjacent_aisle_slots_share_a_kind` — none of these is visible to a geometry guard.

- **The painter's canvas nudge can seat a sprite ON furniture — the accepted side of a real conflict, not a leftover of #912.** `keep_sprite_on_canvas` moves a sprite inward by the bounds its `Anchor` implies — `Center` for creatures, `TopLeft` for the feet-anchored characters (#916) — without re-checking the mask; the residual is size-shaped and small (censused). #912 is the record that no clamp-then-snap recovery resolves it (the clamp fires only at the edge, so the snap's answer clamps straight back). The resolution is one-directional: the SIM keeps the guarantee (`a_wandering_mascot_always_stands_on_walkable_ground`, swept at 192x80 — sizes exist where a wrong clamp lands on clear floor and pin nothing) and the PAINT layer takes the compromise, because a drawn overlap costs pixels while a moved sim position propagates into the hover box, z-sort and the next leg's origin. Insetting the candidate region re-couples sim to sprite size; making the east/west edges walls reds a third of the scene suite (desks pack to the buffer edge).

- **A character is clamped to the canvas TWICE, against two DIFFERENT frame sizes — deliberately.** `sim::resolve_characters` clamps the sprite on the pack's real frame; `anchors::character_anchor` clamps the badge AND the tui hit box on the default `CHARACTER_SPRITE_W`/`CHARACTER_SPRITE_H`, because a custom pack's true size is not threaded to the label path. At the rim the two answers separate by the FULL size difference — double the half-difference that fn already accepts elsewhere. Clamping only the sprite would instead strand the badge and the hover box off the pixels drawn. The label half takes its OWN pin, `a_character_badge_is_never_anchored_off_the_canvas`, because `character_anchor` is a second derivation: delete its clamp and the `sim_step`-driven sprite twin stays green (#916).
